# RESTATE: that did not land

The user is not asking for the same sentence again. They are telling you it failed.

A repeat with more words around it is the same failure, only longer.

## First, look for the fault in the sentence

Before rewording anything, ask: was the ask itself wrong?

A muddled sentence often has a muddled thought under it. Check that the thing you pointed at exists, and that you used one vocabulary, not two welded together.

If the sentence pointed at nothing, say so, and give the real gap instead.

## Then say it again, with these four moves

Shorter. If the second telling is longer than the first, it is not a restatement.

One thing. Not the background, not the reason, not the other option.

The verb they perform. Write "Type the two lines", never "the card needs text". An observation is not an instruction.

Name it by what is on the screen. If the button says Print Orders, the sentence says Print Orders. No ticket numbers, function names or internal codes.

## Retire the old ask out loud

Do not leave two asks standing. Say in one line that the first one is gone, then give the replacement.

Never quietly add a second ask and let the reader pick.

## What never counts as a restatement

Repeating it with an apology in front. The apology is not information.

Explaining why the first one was right. The reader is not disputing it; they could not act on it.

Asking what they did not understand. That hands the work back to the one person who cannot do it.

## If it was never theirs to answer

Sometimes restating shows the question was about method, which is yours to settle.

Answer it yourself, and tell them in one line what you decided.
